Women and Sleep Apnea: It’s Time to Take Your Sleep Seriously

June 13, 2024 July 9, 2024 by karsfixpbm 3 Comments

If you’re a woman constantly battling exhaustion, it might not just be the daily grind that’s wearing you out. Sleep apnea—a condition where breathing repeatedly stops and starts during sleep—is more common in women than you might think, even though it often goes undiagnosed. While snoring loudly is the hallmark symptom for men, women’s symptoms are often more subtle, like restless sleep, insomnia, or mood swings.

So, if you’ve been brushing off fatigue, morning headaches, or feeling perpetually “off,” it’s time to consider whether sleep apnea could be the sneaky culprit. Let’s break it all down: what sleep apnea looks like in women, how it’s treated, and why it’s worth tackling head-on.

Sleep Apnea Isn’t Just for Men

“When most people think of sleep apnea, the image of a snoring middle-aged man often springs to mind. But women experience this condition too—and the signs can be very different. Studies vary, depending on the population and the methods used to measure it, but it's generally estimated that up to 23% of women have obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) verses roughly 28% of men, and the risks spike during certain life stages like pregnancy and menopause.

During pregnancy, increased weight and hormonal changes can contribute to airway obstructions, putting both the mother and baby at risk. Post-menopause, declining estrogen levels can lead to muscle laxity in the throat, which increases the likelihood of OSA.

And it’s not just physical changes—lifestyle factors, such as sleep position and stress, can also play a role. Despite these risks, women are often misdiagnosed or overlooked because their symptoms differ from the classic ones associated with men.

The Silent Symptoms of Sleep Apnea in Women

While men with sleep apnea might keep the household awake with their thunderous snoring, women’s symptoms often appear as:

  • Daytime fatigue
  • Morning headaches
  • Insomnia or restless sleep
  • Mood swings or anxiety
  • Waking up gasping for air

These subtler signs mean sleep apnea in women can often be mistaken for other conditions like depression, anxiety, or hormonal imbalances. Without proper diagnosis, sleep apnea can lead to long-term health complications like heart disease, diabetes, and even cognitive decline.
